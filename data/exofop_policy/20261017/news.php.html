July 31, 2025

ExoFOP has increased its functionality to better support planets and planet candidates beyond TESS, Kepler, and K2. All planet candidates now have a field for the discovery data source; this will be set to TESS by default for CTOI submissions, but can also be changed to reflect the project that collected the data leading to the detection of the planet candidate (for example, HARPS or WASP). In addition, there is now an optional field for a new candidate name, allowing for new planet candidates to be associated with names beyond the standard TIC ID format that ExoFOP uses. These new candidates will be known as Community Candidates. Find the list here or click on "List of Community Candidates" in the PLANETS box on the ExoFOP home pag. This table will list CTOIs as well as non-TESS candidates.

For ExoFOP users carrying out follow-up observations or calculations, it will also now be possible to not only submit follow up observations and parameters to be associated with TOIs and CTOIs, but also for any other planet candidates submitted to ExoFOP, and any confirmed planets that are listed on the NASA Exoplanet Archive.

For TOI-specific work, we are also adding another quantity that the ExoFOP calculates for all TOIs. In addition to the existing columns of the predicted planet mass, transmission spectroscopy metric (TSM), and emission spectroscopy metric (ESM), we will also now be calculating a predicted radial velocity semi-amplitude (PREDRVSA), presuming the predicted planet mass is in a circular, non-inclined orbit.

  • Candidates for TESS and other DISCOVERY sources are now allowed. Naming convention still follows the format of TIC_ID.xx
  • An additional project Candidate Name can be added to a candidate (TESS or other DISCOVERY)
  • Planet parameters are allowed for existing: TOIs, Candidates (TESS or other DISCOVERY), or Confirmed planets (as recognized by the NASA Exoplanet Archive)
  • Adding a new candidate and adding new planet parameters to an existing candidate are combined into a single form from the target overview page ("Add new Community Planet Candidates or Planet Parameters").
  • Bulk uploads of planet parameters can now include DISCOVERY, and/or Candidate Name for a new candidate and planet parameters can now be added for a Confirmed planet.
  • TFOPWG dispositions and priorities only apply to TESS Candidates
  • Candidate Names can be searched for from the "Go to Target" box on the main page
  • Documentation is embedded within the forms for uploads

June 14, 2021

A new feature called Saved Searches allows you to save the parameters you enter on the Search TOIs page so that you can execute the same search later without having to re-enter the parameters. This should be useful for searches you do repeatedly and can be accessed programmatically via URL (described below).

To save a search, make sure you are logged in to ExoFOP-TESS and perform a search on the Search TOIs page. Above the Search Results table, you will see a button for 'New! Save Search'. Click this button and enter a short name/description for your search. Your search parameters and included columns will be stored separately in the database, so you don't need to include those in your description. Click the Save button and the resulting window will tell you how to access your Saved Searches: in the Search dropdown menu in the black bar at the top of the page, in the TOIS/CTOIS box on the ExoFOP-TESS home page, or on the Search TOIs page itself.

On your Saved Searches page, you can re-execute any search by pressing the Search button. You can also construct a URL which will return the search results in JSON, CSV, or pipe-delimited format using the following syntax:

https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/exofop_tess_search.php?id=ID&format=FORMAT

where ID is the ID of your search (listed in the left-hand column of the table) and FORMAT is json, csv, or pipe. You can also use the JSON, CSV, and Pipe buttons to generate those URLs, or use the Delete button to delete a search.

If you want to access the URL programmatically, make sure you are logged in by following the instructions for setting cookies here:

https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/Introduction_to_ExoFOP_php_functions.php#propaccess

July 29, 2019

The ExoFOP-TESS site enables the community to identify objects of interest that may not have been identified by the TESS project as a TESS Object of Interest (TOI). Identifying these Community TOIs (CTOIs) is an important contribution by the community.

To help make the CTOIs as useful as possible for follow-up by the community and the potential adoption of these CTOIs by the TESS project as TOIs, we have instituted a series of guidelines for the creation of CTOIs:

https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/candidate_help.php

For transiting planetary candidates, the orbital period, transit midpoint time, and transit depth are the three most basic parameters to report. Without these three parameters, there can be no effective follow-up by anyone in the community. Thus, the new guidelines require at least two of these three parameters to be included in the creation of the CTOI. Older CTOIs that do not meet this requirement may be removed.

For individuals who have already created CTOIs that do not meet this minimal requirement, we ask that you please go to ExoFOP-TESS and add proper periods, epochs and depths to the CTOIs that you created. For those CTOIs that remain without at least two of these three parameters, the CTOIs will be removed from ExoFOP-TESS. The deadline for updating your CTOIs is Sunday, August 11.

July 18, 2017

NExScI and the NASA Exoplanet Archive are pleased to announce the release of the ExoFOP-TESS website. This website is designed to optimize resources and facilitate collaboration in follow-up studies of targets observed by the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS), an Explorer-class mission led by MIT.

The ExoFOP-TESS site is open to the entire community. In this initial release, the site consists of overview pages for each of the 10.8 million stars in the TESS Candidate Target List (CTL). These pages contain data from the TESS Input Catalog (TIC) v5, including coordinates, magnitudes, and stellar parameters. When the star is known to host confirmed planets, planet parameters are populated from the default parameters at the NASA Exoplanet Archive. The pages also contain external links to the NASA Exoplanet Archive, the IRSA finding chart, SIMBAD, MAST, Keck Observatory Archive, exoplanets.org, and exoplanets.eu.

All content can be viewed without logging in. The ability to upload data and parameters and to search the CTL targets will be coming soon.

May 4, 2015

NExScI and the Exoplanet Archive are pleased to announce the release of an expanded version of the Kepler Community Follow-up Observation Program (CFOP) website. This new service is called ExoFOP and, in addition to continuing to support Kepler via the original CFOP website, now includes the K2 targets, and will eventually be expanded to support future missions.

The ExoFOP-K2 site is open to the entire community. Currently the site contains magnitudes, finding charts, and nearby source lists for the targets from Campaigns 0-4 and the engineering campaign. A new feature allows users to designate possible planetary candidates, false positives and eclipsing binaries. ExoFOP-K2 will allow detailed searches of K2 targets similar to the CFOP search pages.

All content can be viewed and downloaded without logging in. A username and password are only required if you wish to upload data, notes, or files. Your existing CFOP user name and password will work on ExoFOP-K2.
