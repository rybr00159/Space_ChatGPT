# Version 3 refresh of the first detailed review batch

The exact original 187-star selection was reprocessed with the version 3 raw-variability diagnostics. All 187 completed with zero errors. The old queue, checkpoint, rankings, and all 187 JSON dossiers are preserved in `batch_history/raw-aperture-all-orbital-phase-v2_aee6b448959c/`. Original FITS and scientific arrays were retained.

The version 3 code SHA-256 is `23c89080f4f10ef59b79d2896c86edb98dafeecc14081feb2c2a5c69fb37f333`. The broad survey's frozen screening code remains unchanged. Raw SAP orbital and half-period sine/cosine fits exclude generous primary, half-phase, and selected off-phase secondary windows. Fits use robust clipping, a residual-scatter error floor, and covariance inflation. Coefficients and covariance matrices are saved. A conditional harmonic S/N above 5 and amplitude above half the local primary depth raises a warning. This is a diagnostic warning, not a binary confirmation or a survey-wide false-alarm probability.

Eighteen stars received a raw orbital or half-period warning. For TIC 308600649, processed photometry had concealed a raw half-period modulation of **2759 ± 61 ppm**, compared with a local primary depth of about 4184 ppm. Its conditional amplitude S/N is 45.4. Current Gaia radial-velocity variability and a nearby source provide further counterevidence in the separate catalog report. Its initial clean processed-transit assessment should not be promoted.

Warnings overlap: 72 targets weakened under independent raw local baselines; 74 had QLP centroid warnings; 60 had aperture-dependent depths; 36 had a significant secondary selected across the full orbital phase; 34 had a significant half-phase secondary; and 27 lacked three independently sampled positive events. These diagnostics explain the negative screening decisions, but are not formal classifications or population occurrence rates.

Eleven first-sector reports remained free of the automated warnings. They are weak or broad events and are **not credible new planetary candidates**. Current stellar properties overturn the old dwarf-radius interpretation for at least seven: TIC 441386747, 406280158, 271697430, 260352037, 79354493, 231825824, and 259901286 have radii around 10–12 solar radii in current TIC/Gaia evidence. Current stellar properties must be checked before physical planet-size or duration arguments.

Brief follow-up QA processed 57 cached observations for four of those giants. Each sector had a free local transit epoch and a local period search; no rejection relies solely on extrapolating an imprecise sector-1 ephemeris. The recurring approximately 0.88-day signal in TIC 260352037 appears throughout 25 sectors, but a 3-solar-mass orbit at that period has a semimajor axis of about 5.57 solar radii, inside its cataloged 10.11-solar-radius photosphere. Giant variability or a blended source is required before an external planet on the identified giant can be considered. Other leads weakened or varied across the additional sectors. See `giant_survivors/README.md` and its reproducible QA script, source hashes, event measurements, and representative plots.

The earlier strongly recurring TIC 370247818 and TIC 141980042 signals are also excluded from planet promotion: combining sectors exposes recurring secondary eclipses. In TIC 370247818, the secondary occurs at phase 0.47546, so a check restricted to exactly half phase missed it. The multi-sector reports preserve this counterevidence.

The next review must use the full-survey shortlist with current stellar properties, preserve the source-evidence identifiers and method hashes, and independently examine any surviving signals. The automatic ranking remains a queue for investigation. No result here establishes confirmation or novelty.
