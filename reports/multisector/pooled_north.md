## Important limits

- Original sectors 83 and 86 were both used to select the period and epoch. They are not independent recoveries. New sectors 81/82/84 are genuine additional data, evaluated without sector-specific retuning. Long-baseline period uncertainty can still cause phase drift.
- Generic variability flags are not automatic planet rejections. In particular, TIC359679661 and TIC366490373 retain some weak event support and are recorded as unresolved, variability-contaminated leads.
- The frozen eighth-order harmonic extrapolation can be unstable inside excluded event windows. Its occasionally huge residual scores are not used as evidence. Native-cadence out-of-event periodograms provide a separate descriptive variability check.
- TIC287086419 S82 returned HTTP404 and is preserved as unavailable. S81 was retrieved and verified but has zero strictly contiguous locally bracketed events. A wrapper preserves this result and median-window measurements because the frozen helper does not handle depth=None in its final flag guard. No helper source was changed.

## Isolated event in TIC353933809

The S86 dip at BTJD3637.21848 has local corrected/raw depths925/1007ppm, while the two S83 events are much shallower and fresh S82/S84 show no corresponding dips. Its proposed18.9784-day period therefore is not supported. A same-time check read and verified all79 retained S86 camera4/CCD4 products;77 comparison curves covered the target interval sufficiently for a target-excluded median control.

After scaling that control outside the selected event, conditional corrected/raw event depths are 829/831ppm, S/N 10.64/10.56. This test does **not** establish a shared median instrumental cause. The event remains an unexplained single broad dip, not a planet candidate. Gaia recognition separately identifies a known stellar orbit and a nearby comoving source; source localization and the published binary geometry must be considered before any physical interpretation.

## Native-pixel follow-up of the isolated dip

[The full isolated-event assessment](../../candidates/multisector/tic353933809/isolated_s86/README.md) preserves raw 9×9 TESSCut data and failed/alternative fits. Subtracting a peripheral sky median or plane and changing local baseline width alters the fitted depth, duration, timing and sometimes sign. Selected-template native difference scores are only about4–5.3. Maps have positive and negative stellar lobes and poor Gaussian fits; no source position is claimed. A reference-image plus gradient decomposition yields only about1.2-sigma nominal flux-decrement coefficients with residual-scaled diagonal errors. This is processing sensitivity, not a calibrated false-alarm probability or proof that every possible astrophysical event is absent.
