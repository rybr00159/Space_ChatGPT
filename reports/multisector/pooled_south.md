# Southern pooled-search follow-up

The six southern leads examined here do **not** currently support a credible new planet candidate. The strongest recurring signal is already reported, while the remaining selected periods fail local robustness, additional-sector recurrence, or both. Native images also reveal a strong common-background alternative for the apparent highest-score sparse event. These results do not establish that the stars have no planets.

This phase tested five producer-error leads from the completed Sector 95/98 pooled search and one additional mixed-error lead. It examined **24 distinct target-sector observations**: 12 discovery observations and 12 additional observations retrieved from MAST. There are 23 complete diagnostic sets and one explicitly incomplete, zero-observed-event case. Alternate periods, detrenders, pixel regressions and common-period fits are repeated tests of these data, not additional observations.

## Frozen-ephemeris tests

The period, epoch and duration are selected once from the blind pooled table. Additional observations first receive that same ephemeris without sector-specific retuning. Each test measures locally bracketed events in raw SAP, corrected flux, small and large apertures; alternative 0.75/1.5-day detrending; odd/even depths; other-phase and half-phase events; out-of-event modulation; approximate exposure-integrated trapezoid shape; centroids and quality context.

## Main hypotheses and falsification

**TIC 375090561:** The stronger 5.424383-day pooled peak is explicitly recognized as a previously reported period. It recurs in all four examined sectors, with raw local S/N 6.65, 7.73, 14.16 and 15.25 for S95, S98, S96 and S97. The 8.136209-day peak is a 3:2 alias, not evidence for a second planet. The independent recognition investigation identifies Yu et al. (2019), reported P = 5.423940 days, and a public SPOC fit at 5.42428305 days. Strong aperture dependence, large modulation and blending remain relevant to physical interpretation, but this phase's key conclusion is recovery of a known signal.

**TIC 293268396:** Sector 95 has a real-looking, V-shaped folded feature in the producer light curve. At the fixed pooled ephemeris, corrected/raw local depths are 2731 ± 194 / 2883 ± 202 ppm. The small aperture instead gives −548 ± 297 ppm and the large aperture −12723 ± 649 ppm. Centroid excursions are 0.00165 and −0.00354 pixels, with approximate conditional scores 10.1 and 14.9. Those inconsistencies motivate contamination or processing explanations; centroids do not identify the source. A single common-period refinement using all four already inspected sectors gives P = 2.59693523 days, but local raw S/N remains 13.89 in S95 and only 2.87, 2.56 and 1.14 in S98, S97 and S96. Combining locally fitted events with an event-scatter floor gives raw S/N **4.23**, illustrating the difference between an attractive phase fold and robust aggregate evidence. The recognition investigator also finds Gaia RV robust amplitude 81.125 km/s with variability p reported as zero, a serious stellar-multiplicity warning; that does not identify the eclipsing source or prove a specific orbit.

An actual native-cadence S95 9×9 TESSCut was downloaded for this target. Eight difference-image variants test linear/quadratic local baselines, native-only versus intersected QLP quality masks, and optional median sky subtraction. The maximum pixel S/N is only **0.21–2.54**, and every free Gaussian fit hits a width bound. All eight variants therefore explicitly have `localization_valid: false`. Their fitted sky positions or offsets **must not be interpreted as source localization**. The initial fitted offset looked suggestive, but inspecting image strength and fit bounds invalidated that interpretation. Raw and background-subtracted maps remain preserved. The QLP and cutout timestamps differ by approximately 19–37 seconds; nearest cadences are matched within one-third exposure (66.7 seconds), with offsets recorded. There is no precise PRF localization, no dilution correction and no planetary transit-model solution.

**TIC 382190230:** The pooled 1.282-day local box score (~24.4) is not retained by raw local fits or the S96/S97 checks. The stronger ~0.855-day family has broad fractional duration: the trapezoid reaches its allowed maximum ~0.256-day duration. Out-of-event raw Lomb–Scargle peaks are near 0.85 days, while corrected peaks are near 0.425 days. This is compatible with a changing or non-transit periodic waveform; it does not identify the physical variability mechanism. Fresh recognition finds already published RUWE-binary recognition, rapid rotation and public SPOC TCEs near 0.8427 days. The 1.28-day feature does not establish a separate new orbit.

The frozen eight-harmonic baseline diagnostic proved numerically unsuitable for this broad feature: excluding a long orbital phase interval permitted extreme extrapolated Fourier amplitudes, sometimes tens of normalized-flux units. Its huge residual scores are invalid and are preserved only as a failed approach. The separate `pooled_south_conditioned_variability.json` uses one or two harmonics, a smaller declared exclusion and reports condition numbers and full model amplitudes. Fits have condition numbers ~1.5–3.6 and peak-to-peak amplitudes ~0.001–0.006. They do not consistently remove all residual structure, so no particular stellar model is claimed to explain every point.

**TIC 349972227 and TIC 149625254:** Neither produces robust additional-sector recurrence at its selected period. Targeted all-sector common-period refinement also fails to recover a convincing signal. TIC 349972227 is additionally recognized as an RS variable in Gaia/VSX with strong Gaia RV variability. Sparse local event support and large raw variability make these poor new-planet leads. Further period aliases remain possible, but none is established here.

**TIC 238182368:** This is the strongest mixed-pool lead with at least three pooled events and positive support from both sectors. Its apparent strength requires caution: S95 contributes only **one** locally bracketed event (raw 5026 ppm), and S98 only two (raw 3014 ppm); corrected S98 local S/N is 1.72. The raw waveform has substantial orbital/half-period modulation, with aperture and centroid warnings. All in-event S95 samples are within 0.05 days of a flagged cadence; the small aperture gives −782 ppm instead of the raw +5026 ppm, and centroid offsets have conditional scores 13.0/20.6. These are serious instrumental/blend alternatives. S94 does not recur at the frozen ephemeris; S96's predicted event lies in a quality gap. Common-period optimization does not establish recurrence and can move predicted events into gaps, which is not confirmation. Fresh recognition gives normal Gaia RUWE 0.961 and RV-variability p = 0.864, no entries in the checked variable-star cones, and a G14.06/G14.40 double neighbor about 39 arcsec away; neither a clean stellar solution nor empty cones prove a planet or novelty. Preserve this as a sparse, unresolved photometric case rather than a periodic planet candidate. A rigorous single-event/alias analysis would need to retain all allowed period solutions and observational windows, not choose the most favorable phase fold.

Native S95 images provide a stronger falsification of the TIC 238182368 event. The difference is nearly uniform across the entire 9×9 field, including blank sky: with common QLP quality and a linear local baseline, the minimum/median/maximum difference is **40.35/41.18/42.50 electrons s⁻¹ per pixel**. A quadratic baseline gives 8.37/9.35/9.98. This is unlike a localized stellar point source. Subtracting the faint-pixel median sky series lowers the median differences to **0.051/−0.006 electrons s⁻¹**, and the largest residual pixel S/N to **3.02/2.23**, for linear/quadratic baselines. Every Gaussian fit hits a width bound and all eight variants have invalid localization. These checks strongly favor background or spacecraft contamination for the strongest discovery event; they do not assign it to the 39-arcsec neighbor or prove the absence of a weaker transit. Preserve the sparse measurements, but do not promote this feature as a planet candidate.

The independent catalog investigation is preserved in [pooled_context/README.md](../../results/multisector_recognition/pooled_context/README.md), including its `review.json`, `target_context.csv`, and the supplementary `extra_tic238182368/deep_context_summary.json`. Catalog absence is scoped to those exact queries and literature sources, not a proof of novelty.

## Reproduction and preservation

Run with the prepared local Python environment; these commands use the read-only discovery database and verified retained FITS. Network retrievals retain URL, status, timestamp, hashes and raw bytes. The scientific helpers remain unchanged.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --independent 2
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --tic 375090561 --stronger-harmonic --independent 2
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --tic 382190230 --stronger-harmonic
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --tic 382190230 --stronger-harmonic --conditioned-variability
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --tic 293268396 --joint --pixels
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 /workspace/openrouter-check-venv/bin/python scripts/pooled_south_vet.py --tic 238182368 --pixels
```

For the other already inspected targets, `--tic TIC --joint` repeats the declared common-period robustness check. That optimization uses inspected data and is not withheld-sector confirmation. Exact source records, raw SHA-256 hashes, columns, original/missing error diagnostics, FITS checksum availability, frozen scientific method snapshots, quality context and individual event measurements are under `candidates/multisector/ticTIC/`. The main results are `pooled_south.json`, optional `pooled_south_stronger.json`, and `pooled_south_conditional_joint.json`. Raw observations remain outside Git under `/workspace/tess-research-data/multisector/`; retrieval JSON supplies reproducible public URLs. No observation or failed diagnostic was deleted, and no paid API was used.

The frozen full helper raises a `None > 0` error for zero-bracketed-event observations. The wrapper retains that exception and computes a local-only coverage record rather than changing the helper or pretending the observation rejected a planet. Missing producer errors are handled by the already frozen `multisector_missing_errors.estimate`, explicitly labeled in the derived source record.

Verification passed: all 24 distinct target-sector raw SHA-256 hashes match their retained records; every primary summary records the current wrapper source hash; the frozen helper hash is unchanged across all six targets; and the wrapper compiles. Both native pixel FITS are parsed and source hashes retained. Public products lacking FITS CHECKSUM/DATASUM cards are recorded as such, rather than presented as independently checksum-certified.

These targeted follow-ups do not search the additional sectors broadly, do not calibrate the complete survey's false-alarm rate, and do not complete the overall discovery project. The scientifically useful next expansion is to screen fresh sector samples while improving empirical-error handling, sensitivity tests and single-event window accounting; repeatedly inspecting these same rejected aliases is unlikely to yield a new planet.
