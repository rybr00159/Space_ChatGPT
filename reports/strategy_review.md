# Scientific strategy review

Review date: 2026-10-13. This is a planning and falsification review, not a report of a new detection.

## Provided Reddit source

The scientific post was read directly from the saved HTML inside `Reddit Post.zip`, with Python's standard-library HTML parser. No HTML, JavaScript, or external page assets were executed. The saved page also contains unrelated interface content; that content is not reproduced here.

The post's linked system visualization, `https://tic4206066.pages.dev`, identifies the reported target as **TIC 4206066**. The author claims a 3.18-day, roughly 500-ppm, two-hour transit with an inferred radius near 1.4 Earth radii, repeating in observations from 2018, 2020, and 2025. A second, explicitly weaker claim is a roughly 11.13-day signal with a radius near 2.2 Earth radii. Comments mention Sectors 6 and 32 and a prospective Sector 110 observation. The post links a preprint (`https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22967456`), published predictions (`https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23175179`), and the TESS discretionary-program list (`https://tess.mit.edu/science/ddt/`). These details are assertions in the supplied snapshot; this review has not independently verified the papers, program approval, reported false-positive probabilities, or catalog search.

**Neither reported TIC 4206066 signal is a discovery target for this project.** Even if absent from a standard catalog, a public report already establishes prior recognition. It could serve as a low-signal recovery test if its data and ephemerides are verified, but it should not consume the entire initial investigation.

Useful lessons are to predict held-out events, inspect quantitative output independently of generated summaries, test that rejection diagnostics actually reject seeded failures, and include all search choices in the false-alarm calibration. A repeating signal and a successful held-out prediction can also be produced by a background eclipsing binary. The claimed roughly 500-ppm depth could result from a 50%-deep binary about 7.5 magnitudes fainter under simple two-source dilution; independent localization matters. A quoted false-positive probability is model dependent and cannot substitute for pixel-level evidence.

## Initial comparison and recommendation

| Search population | Scientific advantage | Main limitation | Pilot role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Known compact multiplanet systems | High conditional probability of additional coplanar planets; known dips test masking and recovery | Extensively searched, and unmasked known transits/TTVs can masquerade as new planets | One or two controls, then a small residual search |
| Known single-planet hosts with newly released/repeated sectors | Positive planet prior; a longer baseline may uncover a weak companion missed in the original discovery data | Requires current ephemerides, timing-variation checks, and literature search | Feasible discovery pilot if fresh sectors add substantial data |
| Quiet dwarfs with repeated TESS observations and no reported planets | Larger pool and less concentrated follow-up; shallow transits yield smaller inferred radii | No planet prior; broad searches create many false alarms; short-cadence selection does not imply little prior analysis | Second matched pilot, selected from archive metadata rather than arbitrary star names |
| Single-transit/very long-period events | Real opportunity for overlooked planets | Period ambiguity, low recurrence, and expensive validation | Record events, but postpone main search until recurring-signal workflow works |

Use two tracks of approximately equal, deliberately small size: (1) residual searches on 5–10 known single or compact multiplanet hosts with multiple sectors, and (2) 5–10 quiet dwarf targets with comparable magnitude, noise, cadence, and sector coverage, excluding recognized candidates. Begin with available SPOC short-cadence light curves because they provide uniform flux, quality flags, uncertainty estimates, and downloadable target pixels. Select actual targets after inspecting observation coverage and current catalogs. Count unique stars, usable cadences, and effective baseline; do not equate sectors with independent stars.

Known **TOI-700**, **TOI-270**, and **L 98-59** are useful *recovery/masking controls*, with identities and current planet lists resolved through live catalogs before retrieval. They are heavily studied and should not be presented as especially overlooked. For the discovery track, a reproducible archive-based ranking is more defensible than invented target suggestions: prioritize dwarf hosts with substantial post-discovery data, manageable variability, uncrowded apertures, and multiple observing seasons. Avoid ranking by whether a preliminary fold already looks attractive; that creates undocumented selection bias. A pilot can be changed after measured data quality and recovery results justify the change, with the reason logged.

## Search implementation and stop rules

1. Preserve the original FITS and product metadata (MAST identifier, product URL/name, TIC, sector, cadence, pipeline version and hashes). Treat the raw FITS time convention explicitly: BJD_TDB versus BTJD = BJD_TDB − 2457000. Retain raw SAP and PDCSAP; downloaded metadata, exclusions, and rejected signals are evidence too.
2. Split at quality flags and gaps, record every excluded cadence, normalize per sector, and compare at least two transit-preserving detrenders. A smoothing window must be several transit durations long and must not smooth across gaps. Do not automatically remove every negative outlier: that can erase the signal being sought.
3. Query all known ephemerides. Mask their transits with uncertainty-expanded windows before residual searching; report sensitivity lost to masking. If transit timing variations are present, fixed masks may leave residual wings or remove additional events. Check every new peak against known periods, harmonics, aliases, and masked intervals. Never promote an imperfectly removed known planet.
4. Initially search periods around 1–15 days and physically plausible durations, adjusted to the actual baseline/cadence and host density. Require at least three distinct usable events for the first recurring-signal shortlist. Search longer periods separately when enough baseline is available. Record the full period/duration grid and all peaks considered, not just the winning peak.
5. Use BLS as an efficient screen. Refit shortlisted events with a transit model that accounts for cadence integration, limb darkening uncertainty, dilution, and stellar parameter uncertainty. Report depth, period, epoch, duration, radius ratio, individual event depths/times, uncertainty method, and the number of transits providing evidence. A radius estimate is conditional on the source and host radius.
6. Compare detrenders, SAP/PDCSAP, sector subsets, and known-planet masks. An ephemeris fitted to one observing season must predict an unsearched season within propagated uncertainty. If the held-out data already influenced target or period selection, describe the split as retrospective robustness rather than independent validation.
7. Apply preregistered promotion criteria only after calibrating the pilot. A conventional 7.1 statistic does not automatically imply a discovery-level false-alarm probability for a custom search. Calibrate maxima over all periods, durations, targets, masks, and detrending variants using red-noise-preserving moving-block resampling or a justified model-based null, and check sensitivity to block scale. Small null ensembles give screening estimates, not precise tiny probabilities. Injection/recovery should pass through the complete selection and detrending workflow, including masks and gaps.

## Required candidate falsification

- Inspect unfolded light curves and every individual event; reject signals dominated by one excursion or quality-flag boundary. Check scattered light, spacecraft momentum dumps, background trends, gaps, and recurring TESS orbital-scale periods near 13.7 and 27.4 days.
- Quantify odd/even depth differences, phase-0.5 and other secondary-event searches, ingress/egress and V-shaped alternatives, positive-versus-negative-event asymmetry, stellar rotation, and ellipsoidal variability. Report uncertainties and inconclusive tests rather than translating low power into a pass.
- Compare individual sectors and reduction choices. SAP/PDCSAP agreement is a reduction check, not independent astrophysical confirmation. Consider alternative aperture photometry for surviving signals.
- Download target pixels for survivors; compute in/out-of-transit difference images, centroid shifts, and uncertainties. Compare several aperture choices and neighboring Gaia/TIC sources. A no-shift result with broad uncertainties does not exclude a blend. Estimate the deepest possible diluted neighbor eclipse and document what angular separation/brightness range remains unresolved.
- Check transit duration against plausible host density and planetary periods. Stellar companions, crowding estimates, catalog radius errors, and incomplete contaminant catalogs remain possible alternatives.
- For novelty, save dated queries/results from NASA Exoplanet Archive confirmed planets and TOIs, ExoFOP TOIs and community reports where accessible, relevant TESS TCE/alert catalogs, and literature searches by TIC plus common stellar aliases. Compare ephemerides rather than names alone. Catalog absence is insufficient, and inaccessible sources must stay marked incomplete.
- Clearly label each signal: recovered known planet, recognized candidate, instrumental/stellar false positive, unexplained transit-like signal, or plausible new candidate awaiting independent follow-up. A candidate is not a confirmed planet. Do not assign a formal validation probability without a justified false-positive model and the necessary observations.

## Useful next investigation

Once the first control is recovered and masks leave no strong known-signal aliases, execute both discovery tracks immediately. Compare signal yield and empirical noise behavior per usable star rather than expanding an unvalidated search blindly. Strong residual leads deserve additional sectors and pixels before expanding the sample. If no lead survives, report the measured sensitivity and move to a documented second sample with newer sectors or a longer-period search. This review establishes no detection and makes no novelty claim.
